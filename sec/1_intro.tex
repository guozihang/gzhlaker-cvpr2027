\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Video copy localization (VCL) identifies the temporal intervals shared by a query video and a
reference video. Copies may be cropped, overlaid, re-encoded, or played at a different speed,
making accurate boundaries essential~\cite{vcsl2022,transvcl2023}. In a large collection,
retrieval supplies candidate pairs and localization resolves their copied intervals. We study
this pairwise localization stage; searching the collection is outside the proposed localizer.

Learned correspondence-map detectors encode frames, learn interactions between two sequences,
and detect copied regions in a two-dimensional similarity map~\cite{spd2021,transvcl2023}.
For sequences of lengths $M$ and $N$, a dense map contains $MN$ entries. Feature interaction
and spatial detection add computation beyond constructing the map. Applying the full pipeline
to every pair therefore spends substantial computation even on correspondences that follow a
clear temporal trajectory.

Geometric matching offers a complementary operating point. Direct descriptor similarities
provide anchors, and robust line fitting recovers regular temporal alignments without a
learned 2D detector. Weak correspondence, fragmented edits, and short copies can nevertheless
fail to supply a reliable track. Historical experiments in Section~\ref{sec:history} illustrate
the sensitivity to anchor density and support requirements. A single global setting trades
the cost of admitting more correspondences against the risk of missing difficult copies.
These records motivate adaptive computation but do not establish the new architecture's performance.

\paragraph{Route before expensive localization.}
We propose \textbf{FastVCL}, a policy-guided dual-pathway network that allocates computation
at the input-pair level. A front-end policy processes a small preview and predicts whether
the pair requires the high-capacity main branch. The main branch consists of a trainable
frame encoder, a learnable similarity module, and a 2D object detector whose boxes encode
copied temporal intervals. The acceleration branch directly matches frame descriptors,
extracts anchors, and fits temporal tracks with Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC). It converts
supported tracks into intervals without invoking the main branch's learned similarity module
or detector. The branches are alternative localizers for a pair.

The policy learns which branch offers the better localization--cost trade-off. During
training, both branches can be evaluated offline to obtain per-pair supervision; at inference,
the decision uses only the preview. Difficulty is defined by the consequences of choosing a
branch, rather than whether a pair is positive or negative. A simple negative pair may be
inexpensive, while a repetitive background can require the main branch to avoid a false
detection. A short copied segment can be difficult even when the remaining video is unedited.
A validation-selected policy threshold controls main-branch utilization.

\paragraph{Assessing accuracy and efficiency together.}
The acceleration branch still computes descriptor similarities, and fixed-stride sampling
only reduces the constant multiplying $MN$. FastVCL targets savings from bypassing expensive
learned processing on suitable pairs. Those savings must exceed preview and routing overhead.
We therefore compare with always executing either branch and with random and deterministic
routing at matched main-branch usage. This separates the learned policy's contribution from
the benefit of merely running the main branch less often.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our design has three components:
\begin{itemize}
\item A front-end policy that selects a complete localization pathway from a compact pair
preview, supervised by localization error and computational cost.
\item Complementary branches: an end-to-end trainable encoder--similarity--detector pathway
for difficult correspondences, and a direct-similarity--anchor--RANSAC pathway for regular ones.
\item An evaluation protocol covering localization quality, routing errors, branch utilization,
and end-to-end latency, with historical diagnostics separated from new-system results.
\end{itemize}
