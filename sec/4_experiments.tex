\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Protocol and data}
We evaluate on the VCSL benchmark~\cite{vcsl2022} with the official validation split
(41,798 pairs; 27,315 pairs contain no copy and form the false-alarm denominator) and the
official evaluator for interval matching at tIoU $\ge0.5$. Frames are described by frozen ISC
features ($d{=}256$); no network is trained, and every decision parameter is frozen. A
prediction is a list of half-open interval pairs with scores; an empty list is a valid output,
while a crash, timeout or out-of-memory event is an error and is never rewritten as empty. The
prototype has no access to source videos or a verified frame-index map in this study, so the
interface receives numeric track evidence only; visual evidence is a defined extension, not an
executed condition. The new ladder in Tables~\ref{tab:ladder-recovery} and
\ref{tab:ladder-boundary} uses this validation protocol. The frozen workbook discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:history} was recorded on the VCSL test split in earlier rounds; it is used only
as a diagnostic and is never mixed into a ladder row.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Accuracy is reported as pair-level precision ($P$), recall ($R$) and F1 at tIoU $0.5$, plus a
strict variant at $0.7$. Boundary quality is measured on true positives by exact endpoint match,
within $\pm1$, $\pm2$ and $\pm4$ frames, and by mean absolute endpoint error (in frames).
Multi-copy behaviour is measured by \emph{coverage} (fraction of ground-truth instances with at
least two copies for which all copies are returned), \emph{extra copies} per pair and
\emph{strict-candidate retention} (fraction of strict segments that survive merging and
deduplication). Cost is reported per pair as decision calls, prompt and completion tokens, and
amortized and worst-case latency; peak memory is measured separately. Every ladder row uses
batch size one and the same 41,798 pairs.

\subsection{Main comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the measured strict row and the three ladder targets. The strict
FastVCL row is the historical VCSL test-split run of Section~\ref{sec:history}. It is
precision-heavy ($P{=}93.94$) and recall-poor ($R{=}14.36$): the failure mode that motivates
shadow recovery is missed copies, not false alarms. F2, F1 and F3 are the target of the ladder
and are marked pending. We do not tabulate external baselines: their published numbers use
different feature sets, pair counts and metric definitions, and no controlled rerun under this
protocol exists in the draft. We also did not evaluate VCDB: the frozen VCDB features and
annotations were unavailable, so its cells are left blank rather than estimated from another

\subsection{Component ablations}
Tables~\ref{tab:ladder-recovery} and~\ref{tab:ladder-boundary} define the component ablations.
Each row uses the same pairs, features and evaluator; only the listed stage changes. \textbf{F0
(strict)} is the frozen branch of Section~\ref{sec:method} with no interface call. \textbf{F2a
(gate only)} admits every track passing the hard gate without asking the model, which separates
the value of the geometric gate from the value of the question. \textbf{F2 (recovery)} adds
shadow RANSAC, the gate and Noul admission, but keeps original boundaries. \textbf{F1
(boundary)} adds C0--C4 choice and dual-axis deduplication to F0. \textbf{F3 (full)} adds both.
The controls are: C1, a deterministic rule on the same candidate evidence (admit when support
$\ge3$ and residual $\le2$; choose the longest verified boundary); C2, random identifiers with
the same call budget; C4, doubled decision budget; C5, boundary choice with recovery disabled;
C6, a threshold sweep of $0.60$--$0.85$ for admission and $0.50$--$0.80$ for choice. C1, C2,
C4, C5 and C6 are executable on the current prototype.

No ladder row has been executed. Every cell below is a reporting slot and must be filled only
from a completed run under the protocol above. We deliberately do not copy strict-branch
numbers into F1--F3 cells, because recovery, dedupe and boundary stages can change both
precision and recall.

\subsection{Historical sensitivity of the strict branch}
\label{sec:history}
The frozen workbook contains 25 no-JEV runs recorded on the VCSL test split in previous rounds.
They share one feature set and one evaluator and vary a single factor at a time, so they are
useful diagnostics for the design of the strict branch. They are not results of the new ladder:
shadow recovery, Noul admission, dual-axis dedupe and boundary choice are inactive in every one
of them, and no decision call was made. Tables~\ref{tab:anchor} and~\ref{tab:support} report the
two groups that matter for the paper's motivation; the full transcription, including the
combined-parameter runs, is in the supplement.

\paragraph{What the diagnostics motivate.}
Three patterns are stable. First, the strict point is precision-heavy and recall-poor
($P{=}93.94$, $R{=}14.36$); most of its errors are missed copies, not false alarms. Second,
candidate supply is the main recall lever: densifying the anchor grid from stride 4 to 2 and 1
raises recall from 14.36 to 25.96 and 31.15 while precision falls only from 93.94 to 89.04 and
86.33; lowering the RANSAC support from 5 to 3 and 2 gives a smaller gain (21.97 and 24.07) at
almost unchanged precision. Relaxing cosine alone (0.60) or adding neighbours (top-3) changes
recall by less than 1.5 points. Third, the strongest historical configuration is per-frame
argmax with a loose anchor set (row 33: $P{=}63.58$, $R{=}56.23$); it is recall-oriented at much
lower precision and is not a single-factor variant of the strict row. The historical rows
therefore identify the recall bottleneck rather than rank methods, which is why all ladder rows
must share one protocol and one negative set.

\paragraph{Pre-registered falsifiers.}
For RQ1 we require F2 to improve recall by at least 0.5 points over F0 while increasing the
false-alarm rate by at most 0.10 points and while beating C1 on the same candidate pool; if the
gain disappears at equal call budget, the decision layer is not the cause. For RQ2 we require
F1 (or F3 over F2) to improve exact boundary match by at least two points and to reduce mean
endpoint error by at least 10\%, with strict-candidate retention at or above 98\%. For RQ3 we
require two-copy coverage of at least 95\% with no more than 0.2 extra copies per pair, and a
measured per-pair cost below the strict branch's coarse matching cost. A failed falsifier is
reported as a negative result; the design does not get to redefine the metric after seeing the
numbers.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
The historical table shares one feature set and one evaluator, but its rows differ in several
factors at once, so it identifies the recall bottleneck rather than isolating a cause. The
ladder is not run, and its expected effect sizes are hypotheses. The interface's probabilities
are uncalibrated rankings, and the 0.75/0.65 operating points were fixed before the ladder, not
tuned on it. Recovery cannot admit copies absent from the strict anchor pool, so F2's recall
ceiling is the strict candidate pool's ceiling. Boundary labels are interval-level, not dense
frame correspondences, so an exact match is evidence of a correct interval, not proof of a
correct mapping inside it. VCDB is not evaluated, and the historical rows share one feature set
and one evaluator. Batch size one bounds the cost claims to this configuration.
