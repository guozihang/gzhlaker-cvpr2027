\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Benchmarks and Learnable Video Copy Localization.}
Standard benchmarks such as VCDB and VCSL have established rigorous evaluation
protocols for partial-copy localization~\cite{jiang2014,vcsl2022}, while recent
challenges and datasets like VSC 2023 and FiGVCL further explore diverse editing
transformations and fine-grained annotations~\cite{pizzi2024vsc,figvcl2025}.
Architecturally, mainstream VCL pipelines have converged on a three-stage
paradigm comprising a visual feature encoder, a learnable frame-to-frame
similarity matrix, and a 2D object detector (or localization head) operating
over the correspondence map. Specifically, SPD formulates copy localization as
pattern detection directly on frame similarity maps~\cite{spd2021}; TransVCL
incorporates self- and cross-attention mechanisms to enhance similarity learning
prior to temporal detection~\cite{transvcl2023}; RTR refines regional
representations for robust matching~\cite{rtr2024}; and recent frameworks such
as iESTA and VCSA further strengthen spatio-temporal
aggregation~\cite{ding2025iesta,li2025vcsa}. While effective, applying heavy
attention and detection modules across dense $M \times N$ similarity matrices
incurs substantial quadratic overhead. In contrast, FastVCL retains the
representational strengths of a feature encoder, learnable similarity matrix,
and correspondence detector, but couples them with a selective model to
dynamically allocate computation, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy at a
fraction of the inference cost.

\paragraph{Selective Modeling and Structured Decision Interfaces.}
Selective prediction and confidence-aware routing allow models to abstain or
trigger targeted verification on uncertain inputs while keeping confident
predictions on a fast path. Recent structured decision frameworks such as Visual
Jev~\cite{visualjev2026} and VideoJev~\cite{videojev2026} demonstrate that
shared visual representations can be efficiently queried via lightweight
decision heads to evaluate discrete propositions or multiple-choice hypotheses
without re-encoding raw media. Inspired by these paradigms, we integrate a
confidence-aware selective model into the VCL detection loop to resolve two
critical localization bottlenecks: binary track admission for weak proposals and
discrete hypothesis selection for ambiguous segment boundaries. Rather than
regressing unconstrained temporal coordinates---which can degrade under severe
video edits---our selective module evaluates structured similarity and geometric
evidence within a fail-closed formulation, updating the primary detector's
output only when prediction confidence exceeds a calibrated threshold.

\paragraph{Temporal Alignment, Geometry, and Deduplication.}
Classical copy localization relies on geometric verification and sequence
alignment over frame correspondence maps, including Hough Voting (HV), Temporal
Networks (TN), Dynamic Programming (DP), and Dynamic Time Warping
(DTW)~\cite{vcsl2022,tan2009,chou2015}. Temporal consistency constraints,
cascading search~\cite{douze2016,tian2013}, and line-fitting strategies such as
SAM~\cite{liu2023sam} further reduce false alarms in large-scale matching. Our
candidate generation builds upon banded sequence alignment~\cite{smith1981} over
the learned similarity matrix, relaxing the strict zero-floor reset along valid
predecessors so that isolated weak frames do not prematurely fragment a
continuous copy track. Furthermore, when post-processing detected tubes,
conventional deduplication schemes typically suppress overlaps along a single
timeline or via joint intersection products, which inadvertently collapses valid
one-query-to-many-reference copies. FastVCL addresses this limitation via
dual-axis deduplication, merging candidate segments only when they overlap on
both query and reference axes simultaneously, thereby preserving multi-instance
copy structures alongside selective refinement.
