\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Benchmarks and task definitions}
\label{sec:related-bench}

VCDB~\cite{jiang2014} established a large-scale partial-copy database with temporal
annotations. The VCSL benchmark~\cite{vcsl2022} (the dataset name; its
paper title describes the dataset and the copy-overlap-aware protocol) adds segment
annotations on both temporal axes and a copy-overlap precision/recall based on projected
interval lengths, together with a common implementation of five baselines: HV, TN, DP, DTW
and the learned SPD. It aggregates per-pair values at the dataset level by averaging over
pairs after excluding degenerate value-1 sentinels. We follow this protocol exactly and report
the four denominators the macro average actually uses (Section~\ref{sec:exp-metric}). The
2023 Video Similarity Challenge~\cite{pizzi2024vsc} evaluates detection and localization under
a needle-in-a-haystack setting with a $\mu$AP-style metric, while FiGVCL~\cite{figvcl2025} adds a fine-grained benchmark and
a metric built on temporal correspondences. These differ in annotation density and in their
negative-pair convention, which is why we do not mix numbers across releases.

\subsection{Training-free geometric and alignment methods}
\label{sec:related-classical}

HV methods accumulate votes in a parameter space of temporal offset and scale from descriptor
matches~\cite{douze2016,jiang2014}. They are training-free and efficient when matches are
sparse, but the vote space is global and boundary precision is limited by the accumulator
resolution. TN~\cite{tan2009} builds a visual-temporal consistency structure over matched
frames and searches for a consistent partial alignment; it handles partial near-duplicates
but still searches a dense candidate set. DP and DTW~\cite{chou2015} align the similarity
matrix with a monotone path, and Smith--Waterman~\cite{smith1981} adds a zero floor so that a
match can start and end anywhere. Chou et al.~\cite{chou2015} combine pattern-based retrieval
with web-scale localization, and Tian et al.~\cite{tian2013} use a cascaded,
multi-granularity framework with a two-dimensional Hough transform---a robust line
detector---before refining the location, already anticipating coarse-to-fine search in the
time plane.

These methods are already training-free, so the label does not distinguish our system from
HV, TN, DP or DTW; what differs is \emph{where} the dense comparison is evaluated. Robust
line fitting is also not new: recent challenge matching tracks estimate a linear temporal
transform with RANSAC~\cite{liu2023sam,pizzi2024vsc}. We therefore treat mutual matching,
RANSAC fitting and banded local alignment as \emph{components}, never as contributions.

\subsection{Learned similarity-map and interaction models}
\label{sec:related-learned}

SPD~\cite{spd2021} formulates alignment as object detection on the frame-to-frame similarity
matrix; the detector is trained and re-implemented as a learned VCSL
baseline~\cite{vcsl2022}. TransVCL~\cite{transvcl2023} enhances features with self-/cross
attention, builds a similarity map, and regresses boxes with a detection head under flexible
supervision; its matching stage uses a dense mutual/dual-softmax rule. Its inference entry
splits features into chunks of at most 1,200 frames and enumerates chunk pairs, so it does not
require a full $M\times N$ allocation for a long pair; its cost is chunk-pair attention,
in-chunk dense correlation and head work. We account for this chunking when defining the
timing boundary.

RTR~\cite{rtr2024} adds regional token representations and a self-supervised transitive-pair
construction, removing manual box labels but still training a representation and a detector.
TCSVT~\cite{tcsvt2024} couples fast library-scale deduplication with temporal-consistency
re-ranking; its setting includes retrieval, and its reported timings are per query frame
rather than a validated end-to-end pair latency. FiGVCL~\cite{figvcl2025} adds a fine-grained
method on top of its benchmark. More recent work continues the learned direction:
iESTA~\cite{ding2025iesta} combines an instance-relation graph with transformer semantics,
VCSA~\cite{li2025vcsa} reduces annotation to a single frame per copied segment, and
MLT-Dedup~\cite{wang2026mltdedup} uses sparse clip embeddings for retrieval and fine frame
embeddings for matching in a library-scale system. None of these is a training-free pairwise
localizer on fixed ISC descriptors, and none isolates the candidate-versus-refinement
allocation as a controlled variable.

\subsection{Position of this work}
\label{sec:related-position}

The closest design to ours is the classical assembly of mutual nearest-neighbour matching,
RANSAC line fitting and banded local alignment, all individually precedented. We do not
present the assembly as novel. Our contribution is the finite-tube formulation, the separation
of candidate coverage from boundary refinement, the restart-semantics analysis, and a protocol
with a strong simple baseline and an iso-budget control. Sampling efficiency has been studied:
Chen and Satoh~\cite{chen2025sampling} report that much longer sampling intervals than one
frame per second can preserve or slightly improve accuracy with large speed-ups. That supports
the premise of H1 but does not test it; we measure candidate coverage as a function of the
executed dot-product budget and couple it to tube refinement. To our knowledge no
training-free pairwise VCL paper combines these elements in this controlled way, but we state
that as a literature claim to be re-checked, not as an established fact.

Two differences are worth stating. Unlike retrieval-plus-re-ranking and library-scale
deduplication systems, we never search a library and never filter pair candidates by a global
retrieval score; the input is one pair and the output is its intervals. Unlike learned
localizers, no downstream parameter is trained, but the ISC encoder is pretrained and
thresholds are selected on validation. ``Training-free'' means exactly that and nothing more;
it is not a claim of being unsupervised, label-free or parameter-free.
