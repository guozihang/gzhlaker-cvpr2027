\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Benchmarks and learned localization.}
VCDB and VCSL provide partial-copy localization benchmarks~\cite{jiang2014,vcsl2022}; VCSL
scores segment overlap on both axes, but interval labels do not give dense frame
correspondence. VSC 2023 and FiGVCL use different settings or annotations, so their numbers
cannot be merged~\cite{pizzi2024vsc,figvcl2025}. SPD learns a detector on similarity
maps~\cite{spd2021}; TransVCL uses self-/cross-attention, correlation and a localization
head~\cite{transvcl2023}, and RTR trains regional features~\cite{rtr2024}; recent variants
include iESTA and VCSA~\cite{ding2025iesta,li2025vcsa}. These systems change the matching or
detection network. We hold pretrained descriptors fixed and ask a narrower question: what two
discrete decisions---admit a weak track, or move a boundary---buy under a fixed call budget.

\paragraph{Structured decisions with pretrained models.}
Visual Jev~\cite{visualjev2026} encodes one image and public context once, executes independent
question suffixes, and reads candidate probabilities from the language-model head. VideoJev~
\cite{videojev2026} scores temporal intervals and textual options against video evidence with a
shared head and reuses visual computation through cached or joint inference. Our interface is a
JEV-style decision service with two question types: a Noul question that returns the
probability of a single proposition, and a Choice question that returns one identifier from a
finite option set with per-option probabilities. Neither type can output coordinates, which is
what lets us gate geometry before the call and preserve the strict fallback. The authors of
Visual Jev report that predicted probabilities can overestimate correctness, so we treat scores
as uncalibrated rankings, fix the operating points in advance and report fallback rates rather
than claiming calibrated risk.

\paragraph{Geometry, alignment and duplicate structure.}
The VCSL comparison includes HV, TN, DP and DTW~\cite{vcsl2022,tan2009,chou2015}; temporal
consistency and cascading search also appear in retrieval~\cite{douze2016,tian2013}, and SAM
uses temporal line fitting~\cite{liu2023sam}. Our local program follows banded sequence
alignment~\cite{smith1981} with a restricted step set and a tube domain; the difference from a
textbook zero-floor DP is that a cell with a legal predecessor keeps a negative continuation,
so one weak frame does not restart a copy. For merging, most deduplication rules compare one
axis or a product of overlaps. A product can hide a genuine one-query/many-reference copy
because the reference overlap is small even when the query interval is identical. We
therefore require overlap on both axes separately, which is the minimal rule that preserves
multiplicity while still collapsing shifted duplicates.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
The closest mechanisms are a post-hoc verifier and a geometric fusion rule on the same
candidates; both are controls here (C1, C2), not straw men. The proposed distinction is the
controlled relationship between sparse support, finite refinement domains and budgeted
discrete decisions at the same measured cost. No priority or performance advantage follows
from this review; the experiments are designed to falsify the architecture, not to confirm it.
