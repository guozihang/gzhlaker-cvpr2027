\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Benchmarks and learned localization.}
VCDB and VCSL provide partial-copy localization benchmarks~\cite{jiang2014,vcsl2022};
VCSL scores segment overlap on both axes, but interval labels do not give dense frame
correspondence. VSC 2023 and FiGVCL use different settings or annotations, so their numbers
cannot be merged~\cite{pizzi2024vsc,figvcl2025}. SPD learns a detector on similarity
maps~\cite{spd2021}; TransVCL uses self-/cross-attention, correlation and a localization
head~\cite{transvcl2023}, processing long pairs in chunks that must all be timed. RTR trains
regional features~\cite{rtr2024}; recent variants include iESTA and
VCSA~\cite{ding2025iesta,li2025vcsa}. These systems change the matching or detection network;
we hold fixed pretrained descriptors and ask what a small additional decision budget buys.

\paragraph{Structured visual decisions with pretrained models.}
Visual Jev~\cite{visualjev2026} batch-encodes one image and public context once, executes
independent question suffixes separately, and reads candidate probabilities from the
language-model head; answer-supervised post-training improves accuracy, and shared execution
reduces warm amortized latency at higher peak memory. VideoJev~\cite{videojev2026} scores
temporal intervals and textual options against video evidence with a shared head and reuses
visual computation through cached or joint inference. These designs motivate direct candidate
scoring, explicit abstention and context reuse. They do not establish video-copy identity:
Visual Jev answers single-image questions, VideoJev is evaluated on temporal grounding and
multiple-choice QA, and a paired A/B evidence board is our input adaptation, not an official
two-video interface. Its authors report that predicted probabilities can overestimate
correctness, so we treat scores as uncalibrated rankings and attempt prefix reuse only for
identical frame sets, layout and model revision.

\paragraph{Geometry, alignment and efficient search.}
The VCSL comparison includes HV, TN, DP and DTW~\cite{vcsl2022,tan2009,chou2015}; temporal
consistency and cascading search also appear in retrieval~\cite{douze2016,tian2013}, and SAM
uses temporal line fitting~\cite{liu2023sam}. Our zero-floor principle follows local
alignment~\cite{smith1981} with a restricted step set and tube domain; it is not a new
dynamic-programming principle. Temporal-consistency re-ranking~\cite{tcsvt2024},
library-scale retrieval-plus-matching~\cite{wang2026mltdedup} and sampling
efficiency~\cite{chen2025sampling} are adjacent; reducing the sampling rate alone is not
sufficient novelty.

\paragraph{Comparison that matters.}
The proposed distinction is the controlled relationship between sparse candidate support,
finite refinement domains, and budgeted visual decisions against the same core and the same
measured cost. The closest mechanism is a post-hoc verifier or a geometric fusion rule
operating on the same candidates; those are the controls in E6. No priority or performance
advantage follows from this review.
