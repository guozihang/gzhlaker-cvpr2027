\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FastVCL separates sparse discovery from local refinement via finite tubes, making explicit
where matching is evaluated and how restart semantics affect weak prefixes and separated
copies. Fixed-stride coarse matching remains quadratic, and lower memory or fewer refinement
cells do not establish a runtime advantage; RQ1 must still test the quality--cost trade-off
against a simple sparse-to-band baseline at equal executed coarse work.

The two budgeted visual interventions filter anchors before fitting and decide whether
adjacent fragments belong to one continuous copy after alignment. Both are selective,
conservative, share one pair budget and cache, and abstain per item; a geometric or
connection check can veto a visual verdict. Abstention is not a global inverse: only the
affected item keeps its core state, so \textsc{on} mode can still change the final output. The
branch needs an external model, original frames and a verified index-to-frame map; it cannot
recover an anchor or copy the core never proposed, and it can delete true anchors or bridge
real non-copy gaps.

The sampling bound limits support inside a short copy, not every detection; repeated content
may create spurious tracks, overlapping tubes may erase the saving, and interval GT cannot
validate dense correspondence, so boundary error is read with recall. Visual scores are
uncalibrated and the $1\%$ risk targets are not certified by small or correlated samples. No
benchmark advantage is established; until complete full-set metrics, independent action risk
and full-cost timings exist, neither accuracy nor speed may be claimed.
