\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FastVCL turns two decisions that sparse-to-tube localization usually leaves implicit---whether
a weak track is a copy, and where an admitted copy starts and ends---into explicit,
fail-closed questions with hard geometric gates and a fixed per-pair budget. Shadow recovery
reuses the strict anchor set, so it can admit a copy that the strict support test rejected
without inventing evidence; the Noul question arbitrates admission at 0.75. Dual-axis
deduplication preserves one-query/many-reference copies, and the five boundary candidates are
revalidated before a Choice question selects one at 0.65 confidence. The interface never
writes coordinates or scores, every output is labelled with its provenance, and turning the
interface off recovers the strict branch exactly.

The historical diagnostics explain why the design is worth testing: the strict operating point
has high precision but recall 14.36, and simply relaxing the support on the same anchors
recovers five recall points at a large precision cost. Whether a decision layer can keep the
recall while controlling the false alarms is the empirical question, and it is not answered
here. The ladder, the eight controls and the falsifiers are specified, but no ladder row has
been run; no accuracy, latency or cost advantage is claimed.

Several limits follow directly from the design. Recovery shares the strict anchor pool and
therefore cannot recover copies that pool does not contain; an independent loose branch is
planned but disabled. The interface sees numeric evidence only, so a copy whose ambiguity is
visual rather than geometric is outside the current state. Probabilities are uncalibrated, the
thresholds are operating points rather than certified risks, and batch size one bounds
throughput. Boundary labels are interval-level, so endpoint accuracy is not dense
correspondence accuracy. Until the ladder, the controls and full-cost measurements are
complete, the contribution is an architecture and a protocol, not a benchmark result.
