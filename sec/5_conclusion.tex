\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FastVCL separates sparse discovery from local refinement via finite tubes, making explicit
where matching is evaluated and how restart semantics affect weak prefixes and separated
copies. Fixed-stride coarse matching remains quadratic, and lower memory or fewer refinement
cells do not establish a runtime advantage. The proposed experiments must test the
quality--cost trade-off against a simple sparse-to-band baseline.

We also specify two budgeted visual decision points: filtering anchors before fitting and
deciding whether adjacent fragments belong to one continuous copy after alignment. Both are
selective, conservative, share one pair budget and cache, and abstain; a geometric or
connection check can veto a visual verdict. They target two errors that fixed descriptors
cannot resolve, namely pseudo-anchors from repeated content and over-segmentation of a single
copy. The branch is not free: it needs an external pretrained model, original frames and a
verified index-to-frame map. It cannot recover an anchor or a copy that the core never
proposed, and it can delete true anchors or bridge real non-copy gaps.

The sampling bound limits support inside a short copy, not every detection; positive slopes
exclude reversed alignment and only locally approximate nonlinear changes. Repeated content
may create spurious tracks, overlapping tubes may erase the saving, and interval GT cannot
validate dense correspondence, so boundary error must be read with recall. Visual scores are
uncalibrated and can be overconfident; the action thresholds are operating points, not
probabilities. Distribution shift in frames or model revision invalidates a frozen threshold
and requires re-calibration on development data.

No benchmark advantage is established, for the core or for the augmented system. The protocol
and controlled evidence remain future work; until complete full-set metrics and full-cost
timings exist, neither accuracy nor speed may be claimed.
