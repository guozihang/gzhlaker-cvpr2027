\begin{abstract}
Video copy localization identifies corresponding temporal intervals in a pair of untrimmed
videos. With fixed pretrained frame descriptors, dense comparison scales with the temporal
product even when only a few intervals are copied. We formulate \emph{FastVCL}, a
sparse-to-tube localizer that separates candidate discovery from boundary refinement: mutual
Top-$K_{\rm nn}$ correspondences propose temporal tracks, dual-axis support checks and gap splitting
define finite tubes, and a zero-floored local dynamic program aligns only inside them. Fixed
descriptors remain ambiguous on repeated textures and on copies split by weak evidence. We
therefore ask whether a small budget of visual decisions helps at two points: before fitting,
to remove doubtful anchors, and after alignment, to decide whether adjacent fragments belong
to one continuous copy. Both stages query one shared pretrained/post-trained multimodal model,
the \texttt{seltive model}, with per-item abstention, geometric gates, a shared pair budget
and fallback of the affected item on uncertainty or failure. We state three research
questions, controls against random and geometric selection, separate risk denominators for
anchor deletion and wrong fusion, and an evaluation protocol that keeps core and augmented
results apart. The branch uses an external model and original frames; no frozen benchmark
result exists yet, so no accuracy or latency benefit is claimed.
