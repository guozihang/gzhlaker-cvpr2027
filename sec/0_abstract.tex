\begin{abstract}
Video copy localization asks for the temporal intervals that correspond across two
untrimmed videos. Given fixed pretrained frame descriptors, dense frame-to-frame matching
and learned interaction networks can localize copies accurately, but they evaluate a large
part of the $M\times N$ temporal product. We ask whether sparse correspondence proposals can
confine high-resolution alignment to a small number of locally consistent, time-supported
regions without training any new downstream parameters. We formulate \emph{FastVCL}, a
sparse-to-tube pipeline: mutual Top-$K$ anchors on sparse sampling grids, piecewise-linear
track fitting with dual-axis support and gap splitting, and zero-floored local dynamic
programming inside finite tubes. We do not assume that this cascade is faster or more
accurate. Instead we state three falsifiable hypotheses---candidate coverage of short
segments, boundary recovery inside tubes, and end-to-end benefit---and a protocol that
compares the pipeline with classical zero-training baselines, learned localizers, and a
deliberately strong simple sparse-to-band baseline under one frozen evaluation and timing
boundary. This draft fixes the formulation and the protocol; every quantitative result cell
